How it works

Three taps from mystery to match

No setup and no sign-up. Open the app and point your phone at the sound.

1

Record

Tap the big button while the animal is calling. A few seconds is enough; WildEcho stops on its own at 25.

2

Identify

The clip is analysed by Perch 2.0, Google Research's open bioacoustics model trained on thousands of species.

3

Learn

See the best match with its scientific name, plus other likely candidates, each with a confidence score.

Honest results

Answers you can actually trust

Most ID apps show one confident name, even when they're guessing. WildEcho shows you how sure it is.

  • A best match, not a coin flipThe top candidate is shown with its confidence, so a 91% match and a 31% match look different.
  • Other candidates, rankedSimilar-sounding species are listed below, useful when two birds share a habitat.
  • Warnings when it's unsureLow-confidence and non-animal results are flagged instead of dressed up as answers.

Good to know before you start

WildEcho is strongest on birds. About 70% of what the model knows are bird species. It also recognises many frogs, insects and mammals, but coverage there is thinner, so treat those results as leads to check rather than final answers.

Bats are not covered. Their echolocation calls are mostly ultrasonic, above what phone microphones and this model can hear.

You need an internet connection. Identification runs on a server because the model is far too large to fit on a phone.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is WildEcho really free?

Yes. There's no subscription, no in-app purchases and no ads. The public server is run by the developer, and the public API is rate limited to keep it fair for everyone.

How accurate is it?

For clear recordings of birds it's very good. Accuracy drops with background noise, distant calls and several animals calling at once, and it's lower for non-bird species. The confidence score tells you how much to trust each result.

Does it work offline?

No. Your clip is sent to the identification server, so you need a data or Wi-Fi connection.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. WildEcho is on Android first.

Who makes WildEcho?

WildEcho is an independent open-source project by Arun Rajiah. It uses Perch 2.0 by Google Research, released under the Apache 2.0 licence. WildEcho is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google.
